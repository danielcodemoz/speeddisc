Running out of disk space?

Find what is taking up space before deleting anything.

What is happening

Drive C: is nearly full, an update cannot continue, or folder totals appear larger than the disk itself.

01

Diagnose

  1. Open File Explorer → This PC. Note free space and capacity on C:. This is your local-drive reference, not the sum of every visible folder.
  2. Open Settings → System → Storage. Let the categories load and inspect the largest one. Check Installed apps and your own Videos/Downloads before considering system files.
  3. A CrossDevice folder can represent content on a connected phone. Cloud files and folder links can also appear in counts. Do not add virtual content to local usage or delete CrossDevice to free C:. Confirm where the files actually live.
02

Fix manually

  1. Copy large personal files to a drive you own, open a few copies and verify the backup. Only then consider removing originals you no longer need.
  2. In Storage → Temporary files, read each category before selecting it. Do not select Downloads, Recycle Bin or previous Windows installations without reviewing the content and the loss of recovery options.
  3. Uninstall recognised, unused apps in Settings → Apps. Do not manually delete Windows, Program Files, AppData, app databases or conversation histories.
03

Check the result

  1. Return to This PC and compare free GB against the starting value on the same drive. A smaller virtual-folder count does not prove C: gained space.
  2. Open the files you kept and retry the operation that was blocked. If space disappears again, record which category grows and ask a technician to investigate.

What changes and how to undo it

Viewing storage does not change files. Moving, uninstalling and removing do change data: a verified backup is your protection; deletion may not be reversible. This guide runs no cleanup.

Optional tools

Optional for technicians: SpeedDisc v0.4.0 Look only reads an inventory and writes a report, but the executable requests administrator rights even in that profile. You do not need it to follow this guide. No new guided diagnostic download is available here.

When to stop and ask for help

If you encounter read errors, unusual drive noises or missing files, stop cleaning and protect the data. Deleting files will not repair a failing drive.
